# Mom Test checks

From *The Mom Test* (Rob Fitzpatrick). Our wording, written as checks.

People lie to be kind, and they mean it when they speculate. Only what already happened counts.

## Each question

Passes only if all are true:

- **Past.** Asks what happened: "the last time", "what did you do".
- **Specific.** Points at one occasion, one number, one person. "When did it last happen?" beats "How often does it happen?"
- **Theirs.** About their day and their business only.
- **Short to answer.** Two or three written sentences answer it.

## The set

5–7 questions. Passes only if it covers all of these:

- **Beneath the ask.** Why they want the thing they asked for. A job post names a solution; find the problem it solves.
- **Workaround.** What they do about it today, and what they already tried.
- **Cost.** What it costs today: hours, money, lost customers. Ask for a number.
- **Trigger.** What happened that made them post now. See [`DEMAND-SIDE-SALES.md`](DEMAND-SIDE-SALES.md).
- **Commitment.** One small ask that costs them a little effort: a screenshot of the spreadsheet, last month's figure, access to the current site. Someone who won't spend five minutes won't spend money.

## Reading the reply

Sort every answer into one bucket. Only **facts** go into Evidence.

| Bucket | Sounds like | Do |
|---|---|---|
| **Fact** | a past event, a number, a tool they use, a workaround | keep it |
| **Compliment** | "great questions", "sounds perfect" | drop it |
| **Fluff** | "usually", "always", "we would", "we'll definitely" | ask for the last specific time |
| **Feature request** | "it should also have X" | write it down, then ask why they want X |

A reply that is mostly compliments and fluff is weak interest.
